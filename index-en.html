Paál Zsuzsánna PhD

The object as text — silence as data

Ethnologist, museologist and curator at the Rómer Flóris Museum of Art and History (Győr, Hungary). Holds two equivalent five-year degrees from Eötvös Loránd University — in ethnology and in Italian studies — both completed with independent fieldwork-based thesis research. PhD in European ethnology. My research lies at the intersection of the curatorial reinterpretation of undocumented ethnographic and decorative-arts collections, visual anthropology, and Italian–Hungarian scholarly relations.

Based in · Budapest / Győr, Hungary Institution · Rómer Flóris Museum Field · European Ethnology, PhD

Research directions

no. 01

The Imre Patkó Ethnological Collection

Curatorial research · 2022–

Scholarly processing and exhibition reinterpretation of 305 objects from Africa, Oceania, Tibet and Asia that arrived at the museum without documentation. Absence as ethnographic data: identification through the stylistic-province method, a network of regional experts, and radical transparency as curatorial ethics.

History of Tiled Stoves and Ceramics

Long-term research

Historicist and Art Nouveau stove-tile traditions in Central Europe, within the framework of the VEAB Working Group on the History of Craftsmanship. The material culture of clay and water as a transdisciplinary research field.

no. 03

Visual Anthropology and the History of Photography

Methodological foundation

The intersection of analogue photographic training (1974–1986) and an ethnologist's career: what photography shows — and what it withholds — in museum object research. Bringing Francesco Faeta and the Italian school of visual anthropology into Hungarian scholarship.
